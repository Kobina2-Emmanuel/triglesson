0 to 4 min

Recall the right-angled triangle

In \(\triangle ABC\), right-angled at \(B\), sides are named from angle \(\theta\) at \(A\): \(|AC|\) is the hypotenuse, \(|CB|\) the opposite and \(|AB|\) the adjacent.

\[\sin\theta=\frac{\text{Opp}}{\text{Hyp}}\qquad\cos\theta=\frac{\text{Adj}}{\text{Hyp}}\qquad\tan\theta=\frac{\text{Opp}}{\text{Adj}}\]

To find an angle, use the inverse: \(\theta=\tan^{-1}\!\left(\dfrac{\text{Opp}}{\text{Adj}}\right)\).

Which side is opposite \(\theta\)?

13 to 18 min

Angles of elevation and depression

Both angles are measured from the horizontal line of sight: the level line running straight out from the observer's eye. Choose a view, then drag the model to look at it from different sides.

The angle of elevation is the angle between the horizontal line of sight and the line of sight up to an object above the observer's eye level.

Look up: the angle opens above the horizontal. Here the observer on the shore looks up at the lamp of the lighthouse.

The angle of depression is the angle between the horizontal line of sight and the line of sight down to an object below the observer's eye level.

Look down: the angle opens below the horizontal. Here the keeper at the lamp looks down at a boat.

Look from the shore to the lamp, then from the lamp back to the shore. The two horizontal lines are parallel, so the angles are alternate angles and are equal.

\[\theta_{\text{depression}}=\theta_{\text{elevation}}\]
18 to 24 min

The sightline model

The horizontal line of sight runs level from the observer's eye. Swing the arrow with the slider.

The two horizontal lines are parallel, so the angle of depression from the roof equals the angle of elevation from the observer:

\[\theta_{\text{depression}}=\theta_{\text{elevation}}\quad(\text{alternate angles})\]

Golden rule. Both angles are measured from the horizontal, never from a vertical wall, pole or cliff.

24 to 30 min

Example 1: height of a flagpole

From level ground 20 m from the foot of a vertical flagpole, the angle of elevation of its top is \(35^\circ\). Find the height to 2 decimal places.

  1. Sketch the triangle: \(\text{Opp}=h,\ \text{Adj}=20\text{ m},\ \theta=35^\circ\).
  2. Choose the ratio: \(\tan 35^\circ=\dfrac{h}{20}\).
  3. Make \(h\) the subject: \(h=20\tan 35^\circ\).
  4. Compute: \(h=20\times 0.7002=14.00\text{ m}\).
30 to 36 min

Example 2: angle of depression

From the top of a 40 m cliff, a boat is seen 75 m from the foot of the cliff. Find the angle of depression to the nearest degree.

  1. Mark \(\theta\) below the horizontal at the cliff top.
  2. Alternate angles: the angle at the boat is also \(\theta\).
  3. \(\tan\theta=\dfrac{\text{Opp}}{\text{Adj}}=\dfrac{40}{75}=0.5333\)
  4. \(\theta=\tan^{-1}(0.5333)\approx 28^\circ\)
